### 17.1 What was run

* **Checkpoint.** `google/timesfm-2.5-200m-pytorch` (TimesFM 2.5, Apache-2.0) at pinned revision `1d952420fb`, loaded by the official `timesfm` package 3.0.2 on torch 2.9.1. TimesFM 3.0 is newer, but its weights are licensed for non-commercial, non-production use only; this study chooses a model for a production campus system, so the checkpoint that could be deployed is the one evaluated.
* **Configuration.** Zero-shot — no training, no fine-tuning; target series only, no covariates; context 2,048 hours truncated at each origin, as for Chronos-2; point forecast = the median, the statistic Chronos-2's point forecast also is. Flags are the official 2.5 example's: `normalize_inputs`, the continuous quantile head, flip invariance, `infer_is_positive`, `fix_quantile_crossing`. `max_horizon` 168 is compiled to 256; batch 24; eager PyTorch.
* **Grid.** The benchmark's own: 24 origins at a 23-hour stride, horizons 6 h, 12 h, 1 d, 2 d, 3 d, 7 d, 24 tanks, 183,165 scored rows — row-for-row the same as all 14 models (`score_benchmark --strict` passes). Scored by the same `metrics.py`.
* **Integrity.** 3,456 (origin, horizon, tank) windows checked for `context_end <= origin < forecast_start`: 0 violations. Failed series-forecasts: 0. 292 of 576 contexts contain missing hours and 17 end in a gap; TimesFM's own pipeline fills them (linear interpolation inside the context, the last observed value across a gap that reaches the origin), where Chronos-2 masks them (§17.6).
* **Hardware.** CPU — Apple M4 Pro, 12 logical CPUs, 24 GB, 8 torch threads. The official 2.5 PyTorch code runs on CUDA or CPU, not Apple MPS.

### 17.5 Uncertainty and volume

* **Intervals.** TimesFM's p10–p90 covers 0.822–0.840 of outcomes against the nominal 0.80 — the third closest of 14 models (worst horizon 0.040 from nominal; Timestral 209M 0.085, TimesFM 3.0 0.063, NPTS 0.103, Chronos-2 0.049) — with a band not uniformly narrower than Chronos-2's at every horizon (0.567–0.675 KL/h vs 0.582–0.688 KL/h), and its p10 is never negative. At 1 d it misses below p10 on 0.086 of rows and above p90 on 0.091 (Chronos-2: 0.157 / 0.093); it keeps p10 above zero on 35.9 % of rows against Chronos-2's 45.4 %. This is the one axis on which TimesFM is clearly ahead of Chronos-2.
* **Volume.** It under-forecasts total volume more than Chronos-2 at every horizon: -12.6 % at 6 h to -16.6 % at 7 d; at 1 d -15.5 % against Chronos-2's -12.0 % and NPTS's -12.3 % (signed, pooled over all tanks).

### 17.7 Compute — measured, and kept apart from accuracy

* **TimesFM 2.5:** 166.7 s for the full 144-call backtest on CPU (1.07 s per call of 24 tanks, 45 ms per series-forecast); model load 2.1 s from the local Hugging Face cache (a first run also downloads the checkpoint); peak process memory 2,752 MB. Each call runs two forward passes (flip invariance) and decodes to the compiled 256 steps whatever the horizon.
* **Same CPU, Chronos-2 zero-shot:** 72.3 s for the same 144 calls (`results/refresh_2026-10-22/cpu_timing/`), so on identical hardware TimesFM took **2.3×** as long. Chronos-2's published 64.1 s is on Apple MPS, which TimesFM's official code cannot use, so the two published wall clocks are not a speed comparison.
* Both are single measurements, not benchmarked means.

### 17.8 Limitations specific to this comparison

1. **One checkpoint.** TimesFM 2.5 only in this section. TimesFM 3.0 is scored separately as a benchmark-only entry (`benchmark_table.md` §10): its weight licence rules out production use.
2. **Zero-shot, univariate only.** TimesFM 2.5's covariate support is an external in-context linear regression (XReg), not a model input, so it has no like-for-like counterpart to the Chronos-2 covariate variants and was not run. No fine-tuning.
3. **Context held at 2,048 hours** for parity with Chronos-2. TimesFM 2.5 accepts up to 16k; a longer context was not tested.
4. **Missing hours are handled differently** by the two foundation models' own pipelines (interpolation versus masking). Neither sees the future and both are scored on the same rows, but the difference is visible after outages (§17.6).
5. **Different devices in the published wall clocks** (MPS vs CPU); the same-CPU pair above is the fair speed comparison.
6. **The same single 23-day evaluation window** as every other model (§13).

### 17.9 Verdict

**Chronos-2 zero-shot remains the recommended production model.** TimesFM 2.5 is the strongest model outside the Chronos-2 family after Timestral 209M and TimesFM 3.0 (§18) — rank 7 of 14 at every horizon, lower error than the incumbent NPTS on every metric at every horizon (a margin the tests establish only at 6 h, 12 h, 1 d, 2 d, 3 d, 7 d), and lower MASE than every classical and trained model at every horizon — confirmed by both tests at every horizon against NPTS, PatchTST-Tuned, PatchTST, ETS, Theta, DynamicOptimizedTheta and SeasonalNaive, and against both PatchTST configurations at every horizon. It does not beat Chronos-2 zero-shot on any metric at any horizon (8.06 % higher MASE at 6 h to 2.80 % higher at 7 d), it under-forecasts volume more, and it took 2.3× Chronos-2's time on the same CPU. Its genuine advantage is its intervals: raw p10–p90 coverage within 0.040 of nominal, third best calibrated of the 14 models before any correction.
