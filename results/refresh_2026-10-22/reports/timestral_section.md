### 18.1 What was run

* **Checkpoint.** `mistralai/Timestral-209M` (Mistral AI's Timestral, the largest of four sizes, released in October 2026 as a public preview) at pinned revision `9874d252ab`, loaded by Mistral's `timestral` package 1.0.0 (GitHub commit `a534bdb`) on torch 2.9.1. Licences: code Apache-2.0; weights Modified MIT - no rights for a licensee whose global consolidated monthly revenue exceeds USD 20M; attribution notice required. Unlike TimesFM 3.0's weights, these permit production use; the revenue condition is the one to check before deploying.
* **Configuration.** Zero-shot — no training, no fine-tuning; target series only, no covariates; context 2,048 hours truncated at each origin, as for Chronos-2 and TimesFM; point forecast = the median of its 21 quantile levels. The package's defaults: symmetric averaging (each call also forecasts the negated context and averages the reflected result) and its own non-negative clip.
* **Grid.** The benchmark's own: 24 origins at a 23-hour stride, horizons 6 h, 12 h, 1 d, 2 d, 3 d, 7 d, 24 tanks, 183,165 scored rows — row-for-row the same as all 14 models (`score_benchmark --strict` passes). Scored by the same `metrics.py`.
* **Integrity.** 3,456 (origin, horizon, tank) windows checked for `context_end <= origin < forecast_start`: 0 violations. Failed series-forecasts: 0. 292 of 576 contexts contain missing hours and 17 end in a gap; Timestral masks a missing hour as unobserved, as Chronos-2 does, rather than interpolating it as TimesFM does.
* **Hardware.** CPU — Apple M4 Pro, 12 logical CPUs, 24 GB, 8 torch threads: the machine and thread count TimesFM was timed on.
* **Sizes.** The 5M, 28M, 123M checkpoints ran the identical protocol but are kept off the benchmark roster; they appear only in §18.7.

### 18.9 Limitations specific to this comparison

1. **A new release.** The checkpoint is a public preview published days before this evaluation; one revision is pinned and nothing here speaks for later ones. Mistral's own benchmark claims were not tested — only the measurements above.
2. **Zero-shot, univariate only.** Timestral accepts past and known-future covariates, so a counterpart to the Chronos-2 covariate variants is possible but was not run. No fine-tuning.
3. **Context held at 2,048 hours** for parity with the other foundation models. Timestral accepts up to 32,768; a longer context was not tested.
4. **The package's default inference settings**, untuned.
5. **The same single 23-day evaluation window** as every other model (§13).

### 18.10 Verdict

**Chronos-2 zero-shot remains the recommended production model; Timestral 209M is the strongest challenger this study has measured.** It has the lowest macro MASE of all 14 models at every horizon, its MASE is 1.85–2.46 % lower than Chronos-2 zero-shot's, and its raw intervals cover within 0.085 of nominal. The recommendation stands because the margin over Chronos-2 is small and established by both tests only at 1 d, 2 d and 7 d on MASE, because the calibration layer, the 45-day operational view and the system design were built and validated on Chronos-2, and because Timestral is a days-old preview release. It is the first model to re-test on the next evaluation window.
